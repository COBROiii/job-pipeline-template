# Outreach messages

Templates for reaching out to people at companies, for a short chat about what working there is like. You send every message yourself. Edit the wording to sound like you.

## Rules
- **Structure:** their name first; say who you are (admit it's a cold message in the full version); **one** proof point; mention the role you're interested in; soft ask for a quick chat. Not asking for a job, a referral or mentorship.
- **Referral or fast-track: never in the first message.** Only after a call, in the thank-you, and only if the call went well.
- **Who:** 2–3 people per company, best first: (1) your own network, (2) people already doing the role (alumni of your university, recent grads in the team), (3) people in the team and recruiters at about 2:1. Sent one or two at a time; backups only if no reply. About 10 new people a week in total.
- **Channel:** small companies (under ~50) by email first (a named person plus the HR/careers inbox), with LinkedIn alongside; large companies by LinkedIn first.
- **Timing:** if you haven't applied and the deadline is more than 7 days away, message first (a referral may then come before you apply). Otherwise apply and message in parallel.
- **Follow-up:** one only, about 7 days after the connection is accepted with no reply. After that, mark `no reply` and leave it.
- **Proof point:** from `facts.md` / `cv-points.md` only, matched to the role. <TODO: one or two of your strongest, e.g. a project with a measured result, or an internship outcome.>
- Plain words, no flattery ("I'm a huge fan of…"), no emojis.

## 1. Connection note (LinkedIn; <TODO: 200 characters, or 300 with Premium>)
Who you are → the role you're **looking at** → **why this person specifically** (their path or team) → the question, picked per person: "what your work is like and what you enjoy about it" for someone with a path to talk about (a scheme, intern to hire, just joined), "what your work is like day to day" for specialists → soft ask for a quick 5-minute call. **Don't say "I've applied"** (sounds like asking for the job; it can come up on the call). Recruiters are the exception (template 4).
> Hi <name>, I'm <your name>, a final-year <TODO: course> student at <TODO: university>. I'm looking at the <role> at <company> and saw you also studied at <university> and went from intern to <their role>. I'd love to hear what your work is like and what you enjoy about it. Would you be open to a quick 5-minute call?

With a shared university, the reason is "saw you also studied at <university> and …". Count the characters; trim the reason clause first if over the limit.

## 2. Full message (once the connection is accepted)
> Hi <name>,
>
> I hope you don't mind a message from someone you haven't met. I'm <your name>, a final-year <course> student at <university><, and I saw you studied there too>. <One proof point matched to the role.>
>
> I'm looking at the <role> at <company> and would love to hear what your work is like and what you enjoy about it. Would you be open to a quick 5-minute call? Thanks, <your name>

## 3. Email version (small companies)
Subject: `<course> student – quick question about <company>`
Same as the full message, with "Best wishes, <full name>" and your LinkedIn link under the name.

## 4. Recruiter version
- **Before applying:** same as the full message, but replace the ask with: "I'm interested in the <role> and wanted to reach out before applying. Would you be the right person to speak to about it, or could you suggest someone else?"
- **After applying:** same as the full message, but replace the university line and the ask with: "I've applied for the <role> and wanted to check whether there's anything else you need from me."

## 4a. Someone you know (network)
No "cold message" line and no proof point unless they don't know your background.
> Hi <name>, hope you're well. I'm looking at the <role> at <company> and saw you're there now. Would you be up for a quick 5-minute call about what it's like? Thanks, <your name>

## 5. Follow-up (once, ~7 days)
> Hi <name>, just floating this back up in case it got buried. Completely understand if you're busy. Thanks, <your name>

## 6. After the call (thank-you, then the ask)
> Hi <name>, thanks again for your time today. <One specific thing they said that was useful.> It's made me even keener on the <role>. <If it went well:> Would you be comfortable referring me, or pointing me to the right person to flag my application to? Totally fine if not. Thanks, <your name>
