# Story flow for answers and cover letters

**Every application tells a story.** A reader should finish it able to say, in one sentence, who the applicant is and why that leads to this job. A list of projects with numbers attached is not a story, even if every fact is right.

The reference is the letter marked `[BEST MODEL]` in `answer-bank/cover-letters.md`. Read it before drafting. If none is marked, follow the flow below.

## Cover letter structure

Every cover letter for the main track follows these six parts, in this order:

1. **Who I am and what I'm applying for.** The role, the degree, and the experience that matters most, in three short sentences. No hook line, no grand claim.
   > "I'm applying for the … role. I'm a final-year … student at …. I've just finished a summer internship at <company>, where most of my time went into <the work most relevant to this role>."
2. **What I've done before: the main story.** **Reuse, don't paraphrase:** copy the main-story paragraphs of the `[BEST MODEL]` letter in `answer-bank/cover-letters.md` almost word for word, with only small job-specific inserts (a tool name, a detail the posting asks for). A shortened rewrite reads robotic next to the user's own wording. The main story is told in depth through the user's decisions (what they did, the trade-off, what they chose, the result in numbers), then the same story with other people (reviews, teams, what they owned), then "Where that came from": an earlier project linked by a sentence that says so ("That judgement grew from earlier work the same year."), honest about what was planned, simulated or measured.
3. **The experiences relevant to this job specifically, in more detail.** Pick the one or two projects closest to this role and go deeper. Put the honest gap here, turned forward: "I haven't yet … but I'm keen to. My nearest work is …"
4. **Why this company specifically, in detail.** One concrete fact about their products or work, found by research, never the careers-page values alone. Tie it back to a detail already in the letter. Location or relocation goes here if it matters.
5. **Parts of the job description, targeted.** Name what the posting asks for in its own words and show in a sentence each where the user has done it, pointing back to the stories above. It can be a short paragraph of its own or sentences worked into parts 2–4. Either way, every key requirement in the posting must be answered somewhere.
6. **Thank you and sign-off, as normal.** "Thank you for your time and consideration." then "Kind regards," and the user's name.

**Length:** about 500–600 words fits one page. Check the downloaded PDF is one page.

Letters for other tracks (e.g. a non-technical fallback) keep the same story principles below but don't reuse the best-model paragraphs; start from the closest letter in `cover-letters.md`.

## What makes it a story
- **One thread.** Choose the quality the job needs most (judgement, simplicity, getting it right before building it). Every paragraph is evidence for it, and the ending names it again.
- **Transitions carry the logic.** Each paragraph's first sentence connects to the one before it ("I worked closely with other teams throughout." / "That judgement grew from…"). If a paragraph could be moved without changing anything, it isn't connected yet.
- **Depth over breadth.** One project in detail beats four projects in a line each. Bring in other projects only to support the thread (part 2) or to show relevant depth and cover a gap (part 3).
- **Decisions, not duties.** "I cut the design from six parts to four, so I…" rather than "responsible for the design".
- **The employer paragraph calls back.** It reuses a detail from earlier in the letter, so the reason for joining comes out of the story rather than out of the careers page.
- **Their voice.** As described in `answer-bank/README.md` › "How I write now". Short plain sentences, first person, past tense for finished work, no adjectives selling themselves.

## Short answers (100 words to ~1,500 characters)
Compress the same arc:
1. One sentence saying what they want or offer (the thread).
2. One story with a decision and a result.
3. One sentence linking it to an earlier root or to how they work.
4. One sentence tying the thread to this employer, using a specific detail from the employer.

Never string three projects together with "also". If there's room for only one story, use one.

## Checks before handing over a draft
- [ ] Can the thread be said in one sentence? Write it at the top of answers.md as "Thread: …".
- [ ] Does every paragraph or sentence group serve that thread?
- [ ] Does each paragraph's first sentence link to the one before?
- [ ] Does the employer part call back to a detail from earlier?
- [ ] Do all facts trace to the answer bank or CV, with planned vs done kept honest?
- [ ] Cover letters: are all six parts there, in order, with part 2 reusing the best-model letter's wording?
- [ ] Is every key requirement in the posting answered somewhere (part 5)?
- [ ] Cover letters live in `applications/jobs/<id>/cover-letter.md`, and are uploaded only as a PDF made from the user's template (CLAUDE.md rule 8).
